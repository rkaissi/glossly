Saved, People, and Profile

Step 28 proposal: everything Glossly keeps goes where its subject is

The four collections go. Saved becomes one library with types Glossly works out (Snippet, Link, Chart, Memory). Facts about a person live on that person, and facts about you live on Profile. Profile becomes two sections: who you are, and how you write. All people, companies, and text below are made up.

1. What goes where

One rule for every save, every memory Ask makes, and every habit Reply and Write here learn: it goes where its subject is. The model no longer guesses a collection. It only decides who a fact is about.

Saved

Things you kept, to find, reuse, or have Glossly know.

  • Snippet: any text you highlighted and saved.
  • Link: a saved web address, like a bookmark: the page you were on, or a link you highlighted.
  • Chart: the chart itself and its data (the table, CSV) and the text it came from.
  • Memory: something Ask kept that isn't about you or a saved person ("Northwind's fiscal year starts in April").
Anything saved in a browser keeps the page it came from (title and address), whatever its type. Any item can link to people, tasks, and events. Those links show on the item, and the item shows on them.

People

What you know about each person, and everything that involves them.

  • Facts: a short list replaces the free-text note ("Leads the pricing project"). You and Ask add them.
  • Related: saved items that mention them, tasks either way, events they're in, and recent messages, in one timeline.
Ask's "remember" and "update person" become one path: a fact about a saved person goes on their list.

Profile

You, and how you write. Read by every answer and draft.

  • Who you are: name, emails, handles, and facts about you, in the same list shape as a person's.
  • How you write: one list of habits in plain sentences, whatever taught them, and examples of your writing.
Replaces four sections: You, What Glossly knows about you, How you write, and Learn from what I pick, edit, and ask for.

  • Link previews, three tiers. (1) The page's own preview (first link card), like Notion and Slack: after the save, Glossly fetches the page's head once and reads its Open Graph tags (image, title, description, site name, icon). Measured Oct 19 on nine public pages: the head arrives in 0.1 to 0.9 s, the image in another 25 to 180 ms; five of nine had an image. It runs in the background, so saving never waits. (2) A picture of the screen (second card): taken at the moment you save, kept only when (1) gives nothing, which is the case for pages behind a login (Notion, Docs, Gmail), intranet pages, and sites that block fetches.   • Links fill in as they're found. People named in the text come from rules at once; a task or event match by meaning may arrive a moment later. Saving never waits for them: anything found after Enter is linked in the background, as the entities pass is today.
  • Linking vs. adding. Saving only ever links on its own: to people, tasks, and events you already have (filled chips with ×). Anything new is offered, never added: an outlined "+ Add" chip, the way Ask offers a task it noticed (decision 78). One click adds it, linked to this item, with Undo; the chip turns into a filled link. Ignoring it costs nothing.
  • Offers come only from what the bar already worked out, so Save spends no extra model call: "+ Add person" when the text names someone not saved (one click opens Who is this's form, prefilled, since people need a confirm); "+ Add as task or event" when the route found a commitment phrase or a date. Clicking it runs the Tasks and events extraction then (the one model call, only on click) and shows what it found, linked to this item, to add or drop. Without a model, rules find dates and "I'll …" phrases, as Tasks does today.
  • Add a note opens a one-line field in place (or press N). The note is kept with the item: Search matches it, and Ask and Write here see it as "Why it was saved". It can be added or changed later in Saved.
  • The bar slot reads "Save", so the router no longer sends a collection list or asks for best_collection on every bar open.

9. Still to decide

  • The page address (agreed). A new helper command reads the browser's address bar through UI Automation, for every browser Glossly already knows: Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, and Arc (Chromium) and Firefox. The bars differ (Chromium hides https:// and sometimes www.; Firefox names its bar differently), so each gets a fixture and a --check case. It's read once per save, only from a browser, and never logged. Private and InPrivate windows are parked for later (Rayan, Oct 19).
  • Link previews (agreed: a Privacy switch, on by default). One switch, "Show previews for saved links", covers both the page's own preview (a request to that site from this computer, like opening it) and the screen picture. The screen picture is taken of the browser window itself (not the screen), so the bar over it isn't in it. Previews are kept locally beside the item and removed with it. With the switch off, links get the globe. Local only doesn't stop previews (Rayan, Oct 19: it's about models, and offline a link just gets the globe). Fetches never carry the browser's cookies or sign-ins.
  • Which links are fetched. Only the page you were on is fetched on its own (you already opened it). An address highlighted inside text gets the globe and a "Load preview" button on its card: such addresses can be one-time links (password resets, magic sign-ins, confirm or unsubscribe links) that a plain request uses up or acts on, or click trackers that tell the sender you clicked. Addresses on this computer or the local network (localhost, 10.x, 192.168.x, names without a dot) are never fetched.
  • Existing data (agreed): items keep their text and lose their collection name; Ask's memories about you move to Profile, and those about a saved person move to that person (a one-time pass with the Fast model, or left as Memories if there's no model). Person notes split into facts by line.
  • User-made groups (projects you create, none by default) are parked for later, in the handoff.
